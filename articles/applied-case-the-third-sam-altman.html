Applied Case: The Third Sam Altman

Published 2026-11-05 · Updated 2026-11-05 · Aidan Lawson
Tags: Applied Case · Modal Path Ethics
Mirror generated 2026-11-07T06:29:33.659Z

There is an unidentified Sam Altman loose in San Francisco.

We have reason to believe he was last seen outside the residence of Sam Altman, carrying Sam Altman's backpack, walking like Sam Altman, and waving at Sam Altman.

Andrew Garfield says it wasn't him.

The Incident.

In October, Sam Altman told Vanity Fair about an incredible encounter he had outside his home.

Luca Guadagnino's film Artificial, starring Andrew Garfield as Sam Altman, had been shooting on his street.

Altman says he was going out for coffee when he came upon the production.

There were cameras. There were people.

And there was a man dressed exactly like Sam Altman, carrying the same backpack, performing Sam Altman's distinctive walk with incredible fidelity.

Altman described feeling as though he had encountered a glitch in the simulation.

These two men waved at each other.

An ordinary person would likely conclude that Sam Altman had encountered Andrew Garfield playing Sam Altman in a film about Sam Altman.

Sam Altman concluded this too.

Unfortunately, Andrew Garfield has now denied being involved.

Asked by GQ about the encounter, Garfield replied:

"I wasn't there."

He went further.

He had never seen Altman in person. He had never waved at him. He confirmed that the production had filmed outside Altman's house, but said the crew had been told Altman wasn't home.

Garfield also denied having a body double on the set that day.

An OpenAI representative subsequently confirmed that Altman had been home and had waved at Garfield.

This leaves us with an unusually clean disagreement.

  • Sam Altman claims to have seen Andrew Garfield pretending to be Sam Altman.
  • Andrew Garfield claims he was not there pretending to be Sam Altman.
    • OpenAI claims Andrew Garfield was definitely there pretending to be Sam Altman, and that Sam Altman saw him doing it.

Looks like someone has lost track of a Sam Altman.

Modal Path Ethics is on the case.

Suspect One:

The Third Sam Altman.

The simplest alternative to the official account is that there was another Sam Altman on the property.

We already know of two.

  • The first was the actual Sam Altman, who lived there.
  • The second was Andrew Garfield, who was being paid to represent that Sam Altman in a major motion picture.
    • The third was the individual whom the first Sam Altman allegedly encountered, and whom the second Sam Altman denies being.
Who was this man?

Was he an uncredited extra? A production assistant who had briefly borrowed the costume? A stand-in brought in for a scene Garfield was not shooting?

Was he an ambitious member of the crew who decided to do the Sam Altman walk in hopes Sam Altman might like it and demand he become the star?

Or had some unknown party simply arrived at the production dressed as Sam Altman and been allowed to participate because everyone assumed he was supposed to be there?

Consider the security implications.

A man dressed identically to one of the world's most recognizable technology executives reportedly walked around outside that executive's house carrying his backpack and imitating his mannerisms.

The executive himself allegedly saw this individual.

They waved.

And then both parties went about their business.

Where is the third Sam Altman now?

Suspect Two:

The Second Andrew Garfield.

Our investigation has uncovered another possibility, which we believe the authorities have overlooked.

There may have been a second Andrew Garfield.

Garfield's denial of having a body double is frequently treated as eliminating the possibility of another actor.

It does no such thing.

It establishes only that Andrew Garfield does not acknowledge having a body double.

It does not establish that another Andrew Garfield was not present.

Suppose a second Garfield was on the property, independently pretending to be Sam Altman.

The first Garfield would have no reason to remember the encounter because he was not involved.

That second Garfield would have been mistaken for the first Garfield because both were pretending to be Sam Altman.

Altman would have accurately observed a Garfield-shaped individual portraying himself.

Everybody's testimony would now be substantially correct.

But what was the second Garfield doing there?

Was he a rival CEO impersonator?

Was he portraying Andrew Garfield in a separate guerilla production about the making of Artificial?

Was the first Garfield's performance so demanding that the production hired another actor to play Andrew Garfield just in case Andrew Garfield met a tragic end and did not tell Andrew about this?

If so, was that actor also named Andrew Garfield?

And if not, who was the actor portraying the second Andrew Garfield?

Suspect Three:

An Entirely Different Movie.

We must also consider the possibility that a second, unrelated film production was operating outside Altman's house.

Guadagnino's crew was making Artificial.

But San Francisco contains many aspiring filmmakers, some of whom likely possess cameras, backpacks, and an interest in artificial intelligence.

What prevents an independent filmmaker from shooting a competing Sam Altman biopic on the same street, at the same time?

Very little.

Imagine two productions.
  • One is a major Hollywood film starring Andrew Garfield as Sam Altman.
  • The other is an independent guerrilla production starring an unidentified actor as Sam Altman.
    • Both productions arrive outside Sam Altman's house.
      • Sam Altman emerges.
      • He sees the second film's Sam Altman and assumes he is looking at the first film's Sam Altman.
        • The first film's Sam Altman never sees him.
        • The second production disappears into the wind before anyone realizes there were two films.

This would explain every material feature of the incident.

It also raises the important possibility that the guerrilla production was actually filming a third film about the encounter between the first two films.

Suspect Four:

The Big Mirror.

There is a particularly elegant hypothesis involving two delivery men and a very large mirror.

Under this scenario, Altman was walking toward the production when two unidentified delivery men transported an enormous mirror across the street for unknown reasons.

This mirror happened to be positioned at exactly the right angle.

Altman saw himself.

Same clothing.
Same backpack.
Same distinctive walk.
An astonishing resemblance.

He waved.

He waved back.

Altman correctly described the encounter as feeling like a glitch in the simulation because, from his perspective, it was one.

Garfield's testimony would be entirely unaffected. He was somewhere else during the delivery.

The only outstanding question is why these two men were transporting an enormous mirror through an active film production outside Sam Altman's residence.

And why neither has come forward with the truth.

Suspect Five:

Sam Altman's Security Double.

We have all overlooked an obvious possibility.

Sam Altman is the CEO of OpenAI, one of the most consequential technology companies in the world.

It would be entirely reasonable for him to employ private security.

But what if that security includes another Sam Altman?

A professional body double, trained to resemble the executive, wear his clothing, carry his backpack, and imitate his signature walk.

Such an individual could serve several functions.

He could disorient potential threats.

He could test security arrangements.

He could leave the house for coffee when the real Sam Altman would prefer not to.

He could even wave at members of the public, maintaining the appearance that Altman was conducting ordinary human activities while the actual executive remained safely inside.

Now consider the incident.

  • A film crew arrives outside Altman's residence to shoot a scene about Sam Altman.
  • Altman's security team, recognizing a possible threat to Sam Altman, deploys its own Sam Altman to investigate.

The security double exits the building. He sees somebody dressed as Sam Altman.

The two Sams wave.

The double returns indoors and reports the encounter.

Altman, receiving the account, subsequently describes it to Vanity Fair.

Andrew Garfield denies being present.

OpenAI confirms that Sam Altman was home.

Everyone is telling the truth, except potentially the person responsible for reporting which Sam Altman did the waving.

There is, however, a complication.

What if the individual whom the security double encountered was another security double?

What if the production had arranged for a Sam Altman stand-in, while Altman's own security team independently deployed its Sam Altman stand-in?

Two men, both professionally employed to resemble Sam Altman, would then have encountered one another outside Sam Altman's house.

  • Neither would be Andrew Garfield.
  • Neither would be Sam Altman.

And both would have every reason to wave.

The real Sam Altman might subsequently receive a security report stating that Sam Altman had encountered Sam Altman during the filming of a movie starring Andrew Garfield as Sam Altman.

At that point, some confusion would be pretty understandable.

But the security implications of all this are enormous.

If Altman maintains a body double, does that body double have a body double?

Who protects the decoy? How far does this chain go?

Can one decoy authorize another decoy to act on his behalf?

And if both the real Altman and the security Altman attempt to leave the property simultaneously, who decides which Sam Altman gets the backpack?

Suspect Six:

The Temporal Sam Altman.

Altman himself introduced the possibility of a simulation glitch.

We should not dismiss the eyewitness's own proposed explanation.

Suppose the man outside the house was Sam Altman from the future, returning to observe the filming of a movie about his past.

This man would naturally resemble Sam Altman.

He would possess the correct backpack.

His walk would be perfect.

Garfield would have no recollection of participating in the encounter.

There would be no body double.

The two Altmans would wave, each recognizing the other, while neither wished to provoke a temporal paradox by attempting conversation.

There is one objection.

If Altman had access to time travel, why would he use it to watch a movie production outside his house?

We do not know.

But people have used extraordinarily powerful technologies for considerably stupider purposes.

Suspect Seven:

Andrew Garfield Pretending to Be Someone Else Pretending to Be Sam Altman.

We must now consider a more sophisticated possibility.

What if Garfield was present, but was not playing Sam Altman?
What if he was playing an actor playing Sam Altman?

In that case, Garfield could truthfully say that he had not waved at Altman.

His character had.

The character might have been played with such conviction that Garfield no longer recognized the encounter as his own.

This would constitute a new frontier in Method acting.

It would also explain the absence of a credited body double, since the character's body would have belonged to Garfield all along.

I recommend asking Luca Guadagnino how many layers of Andrew Garfield were present during principal photography.

A satisfactory answer should be a whole number.

Suspect Eight:

A Hole in the World.

We have so far assumed that Sam Altman saw a person.

This assumption may be premature.

What if there was a hole in the world?

A true absence.

A null region of space in which no person, object, or recognizable physical structure existed, and which Sam Altman's brain, encountering an intolerable gap in reality, attempted to complete.

The human visual system does this constantly. It fills blind spots, predicts missing information, and constructs coherent scenes from incomplete sensory input.

Now imagine that process encountering an actual hole in existence.

What would Sam Altman's mind put in there?

Well, apparently, Sam Altman.

Same clothes. Same backpack. Same walk.

And the resemblance would be perfect because the missing information was being supplied by the one man on Earth most intimately familiar with the subject.

Andrew Garfield would have no memory of this encounter because Andrew Garfield was not there.

The production would have no record of a body double because there was no body at all.

OpenAI could truthfully report that Altman saw someone resembling Garfield, because Altman experienced exactly that.

The only difficulty concerns the wave.

  • Did the hole actually wave back?
  • Or did Altman's brain, having already filled the hole with a complete Sam Altman, decide that it would be rude for him not to?

Suspect Nine:

The Real Sam Altman.

Our earlier investigation considered whether the Sam Altman outside the house was true.

What if the Sam Altman giving the interview was the fake one?

This reverses the entire case.

The man on the street was not Andrew Garfield playing Sam Altman.

That was Sam Altman.

The man telling Vanity Fair about having seen Andrew Garfield playing Sam Altman was somebody else entirely, posing as Sam Altman.

This would explain why Garfield has no recollection of the incident. He was not involved.

It would also explain the remarkable accuracy of the impersonation: the alleged performer was the original.

But who was giving the interview?
  • A replacement executive?
  • An actor?
  • Andrew Garfield?
  • A second Andrew Garfield?
  • A hole in the world?

Consider the possibility that Garfield, having spent months studying Sam Altman's wardrobe, backpack, gait, speech, and habits, achieved such complete mastery of the role that he later successfully assumed Altman's public identity.

Meanwhile, the original Altman was left wandering the streets of San Francisco, dressed like himself, carrying his backpack, and occasionally waving at the man who had replaced him.

The impostor, interviewed about the film, described this encounter as having seen Andrew Garfield.

He may even have believed it.

This raises an urgent question about the original Altman.

Has anyone asked the man outside the house whether he is still running OpenAI?

We cannot exclude the possibility that the real Sam Altman has been standing out on that street since July, wondering why nobody will let him back inside.

Suspect Ten:

What About Spider-Man?

There is an obvious suspect whom the entertainment press has inexplicably failed to interview.

The Spider-Man.

Andrew Garfield has previously portrayed Spider-Man in several major motion pictures.

We therefore have an established relationship between Garfield and a masked individual who possesses extraordinary mobility, frequently operates anonymously, and has extensive experience encountering alternate versions of himself.

This is just all too relevant to ignore.

Suppose Spider-Man was present outside Altman's house, disguised as Sam Altman.

Why?

We do not know.

Perhaps he was monitoring the production.

Perhaps he had received intelligence concerning a dangerous experiment and/or crime.

Perhaps he had been sent to our world to investigate the unexplained appearance of a second Sam Altman.

The important distinction to keep in mind is that Andrew Garfield and Spider-Man are not the same person.

  • Andrew Garfield is an actor.
  • Spider-Man is Spider-Man.

If Spider-Man was wearing a Sam Altman disguise, Garfield could truthfully deny ever having encountered Altman, while Altman could truthfully report seeing somebody who looked exactly like Garfield playing him.

Spider-Man would also have the ability to leave the scene without using an ordinary exit, potentially explaining the complete absence of follow-up witnesses.

There is, however, a more disturbing possibility.

What if Spider-Man had already encountered the third Sam Altman?

What if he was following him, and Altman accidentally interrupted a covert investigation by waving at the superhero disguised as himself?

I am requesting that Marvel disclose whether Spider-Man was operating in San Francisco during principal photography.

I would also like to know whether anybody noticed any Sam Altman climbing up the side of a building.

That detail would materially narrow the suspect pool.

Suspect Eleven:

Mysterio.

The Spider-Man investigation has produced a significantly more dangerous suspect.

Mysterio.

For readers unfamiliar with this nefarious man, Mysterio is a supervillain who specializes in elaborate deception, theatrical illusion, holographic projections, and making people believe they have witnessed events that did not occur.

He is, in other words, almost offensively qualified for this case.

Consider the facts.
  • Sam Altman reportedly witnessed an impossibly accurate duplicate of himself.
  • Andrew Garfield denies being present.
  • No body double has been identified.
  • The duplicate appears to have vanished without subsequent explanation.
  • And the entire incident took place on an active film set, already filled with cameras, performers, lighting equipment, and people whose professional responsibility was to manufacture convincing representations of reality.

An ideal operating environment for Mysterio.

The individual Altman encountered may never have been physically present.

He could have been a hologram.

The matching backpack, distinctive walk, and reciprocal wave could all have been programmed in advance.

Garfield's absence would become irrelevant.

The production's body-double records would reveal nothing.

Even the mirror hypothesis might have been planted as a deliberately attractive false lead.

But why would Mysterio impersonate Sam Altman?

Perhaps the goal was to convince Altman that he was living in a simulation.

Perhaps the goal was to convince the public that Andrew Garfield was secretly present at a location he denies visiting.

Perhaps Mysterio was attempting to obtain a role in Artificial and had misunderstood the audition process.

Or maybe this entire production was itself an illusion, created to draw the one true Sam Altman into a precisely staged encounter with a fabricated duplicate or a hole in world.

There is one more disturbing detail.

Mysterio has a history of using elaborate illusions to manipulate Spider-Man.

Andrew Garfield has a history of playing Spider-Man.

What if Sam Altman was never the intended target?

What if Mysterio was trying to lure Andrew Garfield into the street by creating an artificial Sam Altman?

And what if Garfield never appeared because, unlike everybody else involved, he recognized the trap?

That would explain his testimony.

It would explain the missing performer.

It would explain why the third Sam Altman has never been found.

And it would mean that the only person who successfully avoided the incident was the one person everybody insists was there.

The Corporate Wave.

There is another aspect of this incident that warrants immediate attention.

OpenAI has confirmed the wave.

This is a little unusual.

This company's representative was reportedly able to affirm that Altman was home and that he waved at Garfield, even after Garfield denied the encounter.

How was this established?

  • Does OpenAI maintain an official wave registry?
  • Was the encounter witnessed by security personnel?
  • Does the company possess footage?
  • Were both participants authenticated?
  • Was the wave reciprocal,

  • Most importantly, what does OpenAI know about the third Sam Altman that it has not shared with the public?

I am not accusing the company of concealing an unidentified duplicate.

I am just asking whether it has checked.
