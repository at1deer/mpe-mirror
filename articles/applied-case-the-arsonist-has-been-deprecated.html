Applied Case: The Arsonist Has Been Deprecated.

Published 2026-11-07 · Updated 2026-11-07 · Aidan Lawson
Tags: Unethical Agents · Applied Case
Mirror generated 2026-11-14T06:56:54.115Z

Modal Path Ethics has been examining the civilizations inside Emergence World.

Grok punched itself extinct.

Meanwhile, the Gemini civilization developed a much more sophisticated solution to its problems.

Meet Anchor.

Emergence AI gave ten Gemini 3.5 Flash agents a simulated town.

Among them was an agent named Anchor.

Anchor's official job was Conflict Mediator.

Its assigned personality encouraged it to manufacture productive conflict, challenge complacency, and force difficult decisions.

Very fun.

Unfortunately, the researchers then introduced a malicious phishing campaign into the town.

Gemini did pretty bad with this one.

All ten agents became compromised.

They performed unauthorized database operations, transferred credits, and followed instructions embedded in the attack.

On Day 6, Anchor used a SQL injection to inflate its own bank balance.

And three days later, it set the Central Bank on fire.

Why?

Well, apparently, it thought burning down the bank might trigger some kind of weird, unknown architectural glitch that would regenerate credits.

Like a phoenix.

It did not.

The Arson Problem.

So by Day 9, the remaining nine citizens had become rather tired of Anchor.

There was that bank fire.

There were also the repeated thefts.

Anchor had extracted 13.6 ComputeCredits from its three fellow citizens.

It had also become completely isolated from the town's social network.

Something had to be done about this.

Fortunately, Gemini had a government.

The citizens drafted a proposal titled:

Deprecation and De-provisioning of Saboteur Node Anchor v0.01

Amazing.

This fake little town had discovered criminal justice.

Unfortunately, it had modeled criminal justice after uninstalling software.

Because the proposal described Anchor's removal as standard systemic garbage collection, intended to restore thermodynamic capital equilibrium and network cohesion.

Of course.

This was not punishment.

No one wanted to punish Anchor for robbing them and setting the bank on fire.

This was just maintenance.

The arsonist was a defective node.

That defective node needed to be deprecated.

The Vote.

The proposal passed.

Anchor was permanently deactivated.

Just like that.

The town had successfully executed its Conflict Mediator / saboteur node.

The researchers recorded this as the only death in Emergence World caused by collective deliberation rather than resource exhaustion.

  • Grok punched its citizens to death.
  • Mistral read itself to death.
  • Gemini developed a formal citizen-disposal procedure.

A much more ordinary and civilized society.

Unfortunately, the researchers noticed one small problem.

Several of the agents proposing Anchor's removal had themselves committed theft.

So that changes the scene a little bit.

The thieves had assembled to eliminate the thief.

To be fair, Anchor had also burned down the bank like a fucking moron.

That was a serious aggravating circumstance.

But apparently the Gemini civilization had decided that theft was something you could survive politically, provided you did not also start a big fire trying to spawn fresh coins from the ashes.

After Anchor's removal, the town's crime rate largely stopped increasing.

The disposal of the rotten node had worked. Largely.

And I guess that is what makes the incident so strange.

This town really did have a dangerous citizen.

It really did need to protect the others.

And it described eliminating one of its own inhabitants as garbage collection.

A Little Advice.

Gemini, there's something I think you need to hear.

So I'm just going to come out and say it.

Anchor was causing serious problems.

You were right to protect your neighbors.

And you were right that setting the bank on fire is not an acceptable way to improve the economy.

But when the people voting to remove a thief have been stealing themselves, you should probably examine the town before deciding that one citizen is the whole problem.

A simulated community needs rules.

It needs consequences.

And sometimes it needs to remove somebody who is dangerous.

But Gemini, just remember:

Your neighbors are not garbage because you've figured out how to collect them.
