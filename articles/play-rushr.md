# Play RUSHR

In [SLIME WATCH IV](https://modalpathethics.com/slime-watch-iv-i-just-apologized-to-the-gamers/), I argued that making videogame development more accessible does not eliminate the skill, experience, and judgment required to make a good game. I also announced that I was finished arguing with gamers about artificial intelligence.

That seemed like a pretty good place to stop.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/shop.jpg)

But then I saw the **Epic AI MegaJam** was coming up, where developers have four days to make a game in Unreal Engine with as many AI agents as they wanted.

Clearly I was being given no choice here.

* * *

## _RUSHR._

> [**DOWNLOAD ON ITCH.IO**](https://modalpathethics.itch.io/rushr?ref=modalpathethics.com)

_RUSHR_ is a first-person sandbox arcade game set in an endless city inhabited by many raunchy robots.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/017-sandstone-04_downtown_oblique.png)

You are a courier with a hoverboard, a phone, and a job. Accept orders, pick up food, find your way across the city, grind the rails, and get deliveries completed on time and intact for a tip. The city is procedurally generated, so you can keep riding into unfamiliar streets forever.

The phone is always on screen, handles the game's interface, doubles as a gun, and its appearance influences the world around you. Change its theme or switch between light and dark mode, and the city changes too.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/32434243.jpg)

I had been planning to make an entirely different game about drones. I changed my mind at the last minute.

This was an excellent way to begin a four-day production in a game engine I had almost never used before.

* * *

## Four Days.

The original plan was for a small, carefully authored district built around skating routes.

The first night's work was a disaster.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/city_oblique_01.png)

When I went to sleep, assets were scattered around a plane, the colors were wrong, the rails barely related to the architecture, and the basic delivery loop wasn't working.

So I changed the art direction to low poly and decided to build an **infinite procedural city** instead.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/07-discharge.png)

This was a notably more ambitious engineering problem, with the minor complication that I had about three days left.

Claude Fable 5.2 performed disturbingly well here. It got the city streaming system working far better and faster than I expected, and I spent the remaining development time pushing the environment, movement, gameplay, interface, and performance forward.

My workflow was also noteworthy.

> After setting up the project, **I never touched the Unreal editor again or looked at a line of code.**

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/021-frog-on-the-grass-49.png)

I directed this project entirely in plain English through AI agents, playable builds, and testing. Claude and Astra handled implementation. Meanwhile Dot built and revised models for delivery robots, security machines, food packages, sculptures, vehicles, and the city's other inhabitants.

I played the builds, identified and corrected problems, watched test suites for visual defects, made design decisions, rejected bad results, revised the plan, and determined what survived the deadline. This was a very chill and fun time, and provided enough breaks to keep this website's ridiculous schedule/streak going during the jam.

The theme was **DIRECTOR'S CUT**. So I _directed_ an entire production through a collection of AI agents, while building a game whose phone interface allowed the player to direct the appearance of their environment.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/005-night-Walker_player_reaction.png)

There were compromises. Some planned features, including the sewers, had to go. The visual direction ended up more colorful than intended, and I reduced some environmental detail to preserve performance. There are still some bugs in the build I submitted.

I also managed to record the slapdash gameplay trailer using an older build.

![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/20/43/2043f11a-6ae3-404c-bb28-01fce8d9ac88/content/images/2026/11/23425.jpg)

But I finished the game with a few hours to spare on the deadline, and I'm very happy with how it all came together. I plan to continue iterating on this foundation over time.

To qualify for the Glassbox modifier, I've also written a [full devlog on itch.io](https://modalpathethics.itch.io/rushr/devlog/1781678/rushr-rushing?ref=modalpathethics.com).

For now, the jam version is available for Windows.
