# Applied Case: The Incident

On November 12, [WSMV published](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/11/12/tennessee-police-make-arrest-following-incident-with-wild-opossum-heres-what-happened/?ref=modalpathethics.com) the report we've all been waiting for:

> **“Tennessee police make arrest following ‘incident’ with wild opossum. Here’s what happened.”**

Finally.

An explanation.

I clicked immediately.

According to the Lincoln County Sheriff's Office, deputies arrested a person following an incident involving a wild opossum.

This person was identified following reports of the incident.

Fayetteville police received several calls about the incident.

The Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency is now investigating the incident.

Citizens were thanked for reporting the incident.

And at the conclusion of this groundbreaking investigation, WSMV reveals that authorities did not specify where or when the incident occurred, what happened to the opossum, or what condition it was in.

I have now read the entire report.

**What the fuck happened?**

* * *

## What We Know.

-   There was an opossum.
-   It was wild.
-   There was a person.
-   There was an incident.
-   Said incident was serious enough to generate multiple reports, involve three law enforcement agencies, and result in an arrest.

We do not know what the person did.

We do not know what the opossum did.

We do not know whether the opossum knew this person.

We do not know whether the incident began with the person or the opossum.

The opossum has not been publicly identified as a suspect.

This is the complete account provided by an article entitled _Here's what happened._

* * *

## Reconstructing The Incident.

Without further evidence, we must consider the possibilities.

Perhaps the opossum entered a bank and attempted to withdraw money from an account belonging to the person.

Perhaps the person was operating an illegal opossum operation.

Perhaps the opossum was operating an illegal person operation.

Perhaps the opossum had been impersonating a police officer — an activity which, according to the available reporting, has yet to be explicitly ruled out — and the person was in on it.

The involvement of the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency suggests a wildlife component.

We have already established that with the opossum.

But why were three agencies necessary?

One possibility is that the opossum had declared itself sheriff of Lincoln County, requiring law enforcement to seek jurisdictional assistance before arresting a competing sheriff.

Another is that this animal was carrying classified documents.

Or the opossum might have been attempting to annex Fayetteville.

And maybe the person was arrested for refusing to recognize the annexation.

There is also the possibility of an incredibly complex financial crime, in which the opossum served as the registered agent of a shell corporation conducting fraudulent wildlife transactions across state lines.

We should not dismiss the possibility of espionage.

The opossum **is** nocturnal.

And it possesses a prehensile tail.

So it may have been working for a foreign government.

We just don't know.

**None of these theories can be excluded using the information provided by WSMV.**

* * *

## The Incident Expands.

The sheriff's office has thanked the public for reporting The Incident.

This establishes that witnesses existed.

Those witnesses apparently knew something about The Incident.

They told that knowledge to the cops.

The police investigated The Incident.

Then they arrested someone, about The Incident, almost certainly.

Then police informed the press that The Incident had been investigated.

The press informed the public that the police had informed them of The Incident.

So the public has now been officially informed.

The information is that there was an Incident.

Somewhere along this chain, the actual event appears to have just disappeared.

There was no Incident, anymore. The world has forgotten it.

We have also achieved an interesting arrangement in which every institution involved can confirm that information has successfully made it through the handoff, while the public remains unaware of its contents.

The Incident has become a self-sustaining administrative object.

It can be reported, referred, investigated, acknowledged, and thanked without **ever** being described.

* * *

## Unfortunately, We Found Out.

There is a problem with the theories.

Other news organizations actually reported something.

At 1:02 p.m. on November 12, more than two hours before WSMV's article was published, [WAFF reported](https://www.waff.com/2026/11/12/officials-arrest-an-individual-following-an-incident-involving-wild-possum/?ref=modalpathethics.com) allegations that someone had livestreamed themselves abusing the opossum.

[WHNT also reported](https://www.aol.com/articles/arrest-made-multiple-agencies-investigate-184144000.html?ref=modalpathethics.com) that For Fox Sake Wildlife Rescue had described a circulating video allegedly showing _severe torture of a young female opossum, ending with the animal apparently lifeless._

Oh.

These are allegations. The arresting authorities had not publicly specified the charges, and the animal's death had not been officially confirmed in the reporting reviewed here.

Man.

Well.

That, uh.

I was prepared to accuse this opossum of international espionage.

It appears instead that we may be looking at an exceptionally cruel act committed against a small, innocent animal this entire Incident.

**This seems like something that could have gone in the other article.**

-   The police may have had valid investigative reasons for withholding details.
-   A reporter may have been unable to independently verify circulating footage.

Neither circumstance requires presenting an explanation that contains simply no account of the Incident at all.

* * *

## The Ruling.

The word _incident_ does have legitimate uses, even after what I've just done to it.

This one allows authorities to acknowledge an event before its precise character has been established. It avoids prematurely declaring a crime or assigning responsibility.

But a useful provisional label can also become a substitute for the very information it was meant to organize.

For now, the opossum's fate remains unconfirmed in the official account.

The investigation continues.

The person has been arrested.

The incident has been referred to the appropriate authorities.

The incident is being investigated.

The incident has been reported.

The incident is an incident.

The incident was.
