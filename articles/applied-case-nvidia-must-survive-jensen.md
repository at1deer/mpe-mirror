# Applied Case: Nvidia Must Survive Jensen

Modal Path Ethics has already [given Jensen Huang an F.](https://modalpathethics.com/failed-field-analysts-jensen-huang-and-the-perfect-incentives/)

In September, I dubbed him a **Failed Field Analyst** after a fairly petty argument over two words. Huang had described AI companies as **“perfectly incentivized to do the right thing”** and assigned humanity's risk of AI extinction by 2030 a **“0% chance.”**

Perfectly. Zero. I had some objections.

I also spent considerable time explaining that Huang is no fool. His account of engineering competence, commercial incentives, and technological progress identified things that were very real. He understood the machine he was describing. The objection concerned what happened when his demonstrated knowledge of that machine became confidence about the entire field surrounding it.

That seemed like a sufficiently important philosophical disagreement at the time.

Jensen Huang has since developed a somewhat more immediate problem involving the continuation of the machine itself.

* * *

## A Singular Person.

On November 17, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella was asked about Huang's possible return to Nvidia.

His [answer to Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/17/microsofts-satya-nadella-jensen-huang-a-singular-person-but-its-up-to-nvidia-to-decide-whether-to-bring-him-back-as-ceo/?ref=modalpathethics.com) was pretty careful.

He called Huang **“a singular person.”** He expressed admiration for what Huang had accomplished, said he had reached out to him personally, and left the leadership decision to Nvidia's board.

Then Nadella discussed Nvidia's leadership bench.

He described its considerable technical and managerial talent, mentioned interim CEO Colette Kress, who had previously worked at Microsoft, and explained that Microsoft's work with Nvidia was continuing. The companies were already scaling systems built around Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture.

This is a _very_ interesting statement from one of Nvidia's largest customers.

Nadella recognizes the founder's extraordinary contributions. He refuses to appoint himself judge of his future. Then he returns to the work that Microsoft needs Nvidia to keep doing.

The language has changed.

Nobody needs to insult Huang. Nobody needs to dispute his importance. A major customer had just publicly distinguished its relationship with a productive institution from its relationship with the individual who built it.

Later that day, Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones made the distinction somewhat less diplomatic.

Jones [opened the company's earnings call](https://www.reuters.com/business/nvidia-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-above-estimates-first-report-since-huangs-2026-11-17/?ref=modalpathethics.com) by telling investors that the board would **not** rehire Huang as CEO and would not take questions about the matter. Nvidia had engaged Heidrick & Struggles to conduct its search for a permanent successor.

That was the board's first direct public rejection of reinstatement after several days of reported deliberations, petitions, prominent endorsements, and speculation.

Huang had said earlier that Tuesday that he would be honored to return if directors decided it was best for the company.

That was the board's public answer.

The argument inside the boardroom apparently continued.

Later that evening, [Semafor reported](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/17/2026/some-nvidia-directors-push-to-bring-huang-back-despite-board-opposition?ref=modalpathethics.com) that a minority of Nvidia directors was still pushing for Huang's return. Huang had reportedly spoken with at least one director about reinstatement before the earnings call. Other directors were narrowing the search for a successor, with Arm's René Haas and Marvell's Matt Murphy among those considered. Haas had reportedly expressed concern about taking the job while calls to restore Huang continued.

This account rests on unnamed sources familiar with the deliberations. Nvidia and Huang declined to comment.

So that complicates the succession. A board can appoint a new chief executive while leaving that executive's authority vulnerable to continued efforts to restore the predecessor. The reported division makes the material task more demanding: Nvidia must give its successor a governing mandate that survives disagreement among the directors themselves.

Jones has announced the decision. The board must now demonstrate that it can carry it.

A company that had operated under its founder for thirty-three years was going to attempt something it had never done before.

It was going to continue without Jensen.

The relevant question is whether it should.

**Spoiler: The ruling is yes. Nvidia should carry the succession through.**

The reasons begin before Huang left.

* * *

## This Quarter Was Already Over.

On November 17, Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, more than double the corresponding figure from the previous year. Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion. The company projected approximately $130 billion for the next quarter, exceeding analysts' expectations.

These results were extraordinary.

They were also almost entirely irrelevant as evidence of successful succession.

[Nvidia's fiscal third quarter ended October 25](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx?ref=modalpathethics.com). Huang resigned on November 11.

The earnings arrived after Jensen left. The quarter was already over.

Orders, production, customer commitments, pricing, financing, and strategic decisions contributing to that quarter belonged to the organization operating under Huang's leadership. Some were products of years of labor.

Those results deserve to be credited to the institution and its people, including Huang. They cannot be used to demonstrate what happens when that institution must make consequential new decisions without him.

But the earnings report still contained something more useful than a retrospective victory.

It disclosed the field Nvidia must now carry forward.

According to its [November 17 CFO commentary filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm?ref=modalpathethics.com), Nvidia entered this transition carrying around **$518 billion in future commitments** across supply and capacity procurement, cloud services, data-center leases, equity investments, and capital expenditure.

-   The largest component was $390 billion in supply and capacity commitments.
-   Another $102 billion concerned cloud-service agreements and data-center leases, including leases not yet commenced.
-   The company separately disclosed $32 billion in conditional arrangements with specialized cloud providers, known as **_neoclouds_**.
-   It also reported maximum gross exposure of $118 billion under various financing guarantees.
-   The largest component, up to $105 billion, supports a multiyear Ohio data-center development intended to host Nvidia infrastructure under long-term leases to OpenAI.
-   The guarantees activate in phases, with the first expected in fiscal 2029.

These figures describe different categories of obligations, some conditional, with different timelines and potentially related exposures. They cannot responsibly be added together into a single immediate debt bill. Nvidia reported that it had made no payments under those guarantees.

The company also held $53.5 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable debt securities, and generated $48.3 billion in operating cash flow during the quarter.

Nvidia possesses enormous financial strength.

It has also made enormous commitments against a future whose actual demand, financing, infrastructure, and political conditions remain in motion.

That is the field the successor inherits.

And much of it was already being reconsidered before the resignation.

* * *

## The Bank of Jensen Huang.

Nvidia's commercial success is among the most incredible achievements in modern technology.

Huang helped organize an industry around accelerated computing. GPUs became central to deep learning. CUDA gave developers a durable software environment. The company assembled engineering, chip design, networking, software, manufacturing relationships, and customer support into an increasingly integrated computing platform.

That work made previously difficult activities practicable for other organizations.

A research laboratory could acquire computing capability without developing its own accelerator architecture. A cloud provider could offer it to customers. A startup could rent access to infrastructure it could never afford to own.

This was a real expansion. But some customers still lacked the capital to acquire enough computing capacity, the infrastructure to operate it, or the financial history to persuade lenders to finance it.

Nvidia helped address that problem, too.

Its investments, financing relationships, guarantees, and infrastructure agreements enabled projects that might otherwise have remained inaccessible. Banks and investment firms could supply capital against a growing market for AI computation. New cloud providers could buy Nvidia systems and sell access onward. Nvidia could develop customers capable of purchasing more Nvidia products.

The arrangement has a coherent productive logic.

-   A financier supplies capital.
-   A developer builds a data center.
-   A cloud provider purchases infrastructure.
-   Customers rent its computing services.
-   Revenue services the financing and supports further investment.

When the customers are real, the capacity is useful, and the obligations are sustainable, this system can generate something valuable that did not previously exist.

There is another possible continuation.

-   Nvidia supplies capital or guarantees to a company that uses the financing to acquire Nvidia equipment.
-   The sale becomes revenue for Nvidia.
-   That revenue supports confidence in further expansion.
-   Additional financing supports additional purchases.

This structure can become increasingly dependent on new investment and future demand arriving in sufficient quantities to support the commitments already made.

The same money-flow diagram can describe a productive industry developing its customers _or_ an unstable financial arrangement supporting demand that customers cannot yet sustain independently.

Meaning we have to inspect the actual transactions.

That is where Michael Burry enters, naturally.

In his November 12 essay, [_The Don Goes Down: What Jensen's Fall Means_](https://michaeljburry.substack.com/p/the-don-goes-down-what-jensens-fall), Burry argued that Huang's unusually concentrated executive authority helped enforce Nvidia's commercial position. He cited reports that Huang had roughly sixty direct reports and delegated little, and raised concerns about customer restrictions, purchasing practices, and the company's dependence on its founder.

Some of those claims concern reported antitrust scrutiny. Others are Burry's interpretations of Nvidia's business practices. The existence of investigation or financial dependence does not establish that Nvidia unlawfully coerced its customers.

But Burry's underlying question is serious.

> How much of Nvidia's commercial coordination belongs to durable institutions, contracts, and relationships that can survive Jensen Huang? How much still requires the personal intervention of one executive?

> And how much of what looks like customer demand is sustained by financial arrangements that themselves depend on Nvidia's continuing support?

There is an especially important complication.

-   Huang was **already** trying to change some of those arrangements.

[The Wall Street Journal reported](https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-colette-kress-nvidia-ceo-eb289097?ref=modalpathethics.com) that Nvidia's finance leadership had been confronting increasingly large guarantee commitments. The paper also reported that Huang had begun pressing cloud customers to obtain greater upfront financial commitments from their own customers, reducing their reliance on Nvidia's backing.

Kress had expressed reservations about some financing arrangements herself. Reuters Breakingviews separately reported that she resisted assuming excessive residual-value exposure in the company's financing platform.

So the necessary correction was already beginning under Huang.

His departure changed the people responsible for responding to the difficulty.

Returning him would therefore restore an unusually capable coordinator to an organization that had already recognized reasons to reconsider its financing practices.

The question becomes whether the correction can continue without restoring the previous concentration of authority.

## What Actually Breaks When Jensen Leaves?

There is a temptation to describe Nvidia as though Huang were an interchangeable chief executive whose removal simply changes an entry on the organizational chart.

That would be incorrect.

Huang spent decades accumulating knowledge of the company's engineering, product architecture, customers, suppliers, financial relationships, organizational culture, and strategic direction.

His personal relationships have consequences.

A customer may accept an extraordinary commitment because Huang has demonstrated technical judgment over decades. An engineer may remain because Huang understands the project. A supplier may coordinate around his account of future demand. A financier may accept uncertainty because Huang has repeatedly delivered against difficult promises.

Some of these relations may be difficult to transfer. Some may be impossible to reproduce exactly.

An institutional organization can depend upon the coordinated activity of people [without owning those people](https://modalpathethics.com/amartya-sen-martha-nussbaum-and-modal-path-ethics/) or acquiring their abilities by changing their job titles.

This is the problem Modal Path Ethics had examined in [_The Firing of Sam Altman_](https://modalpathethics.com/applied-case-the-firing-of-sam-altman/).

In November 2023, OpenAI's board exercised its power to remove Altman. The removal itself worked quite well. What followed demonstrated that the board had not secured the material capacity to carry the organization into a stable successor arrangement.

The authority to remove a chief executive did not supply the leadership, relationships, technical knowledge, employee support, financing, or institutional continuity needed after removal.

Nvidia must face that same test.

But the two organizations occupy different histories.

Nvidia has spent more than three decades developing products and organizational competence around an established commercial operation. It has a functioning engineering hierarchy, major supplier relationships, a technical roadmap, and experienced executives. Kress has been its CFO since 2013.

That does not establish that she can replace Huang's entire contribution, but it establishes that Nvidia already possesses operating resources with which a succession can be attempted.

Nadella's statement is relevant here.

Microsoft has significant commitments to Nvidia's computing infrastructure. Its leadership has a direct interest in whether Nvidia can keep producing, delivering, and supporting the equipment its customers need.

Nadella did not announce that those functions had collapsed. He described continuing technical work and ongoing plans.

That is evidence of operative continuation through the transition, not proof that every future decision will be equally successful.

Existing orders can be fulfilled while new negotiations deteriorate. An established architecture can ship while the next architecture loses direction. Employees can continue working while the relationships supporting their coordination begin to weaken.

We should watch for those.

We should also recognize that Huang's departure may permit other forms of coordination to develop.

Senior executives can acquire decision-making authority they previously lacked. Finance can impose limits on commitments it previously struggled to control. Technical leaders can develop independent strategic judgment. Customer relationships can become less dependent on access to one person.

Those outcomes are not guaranteed by removal.

They are reachable through a successor arrangement that actually distributes the work.

Reinstating Huang would make it easier to recover his personal judgment. It would also restore an arrangement in which much of the organization's coordination had come to depend on that judgment.

That is the central trade.

## The Field Is Larger Than Nvidia.

Nvidia does not operate alone.

Its products are fabricated through a complex semiconductor supply chain that includes Taiwan's manufacturing industry, advanced packaging, memory suppliers, equipment manufacturers, and specialized engineering firms.

Its largest customers include companies building their own computing alternatives. Its smaller customers include firms for which purchasing Nvidia equipment requires commitments far beyond their current financial resources.

Governments care about access to advanced computation for reasons ranging from economic development to military capability.

Communities care about the land, electricity, water, employment, infrastructure, and public decisions associated with enormous data-center projects.

Each occupies a different relation to Nvidia's continuation.

The supplier wants predictable production demand. The financier wants obligations that can be repaid. The cloud provider wants reliable infrastructure at viable prices. The startup needs accessible computing capacity without becoming permanently dependent on a patron. The employee needs an organization in which competent work and protected disagreement can continue. The community needs authority over the consequences arriving where it lives.

A change that benefits Nvidia can damage one of these relations. A change that costs Nvidia money can improve another.

There is no honest way to flatten this entire arrangement into Nvidia's market capitalization.

That also applies to Huang's political relationships.

Washington is divided about his return. President Trump has expressed sympathy for reinstatement, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for an outside successor. Congressional scrutiny and export-control investigations continue.

China has rejected allegations of Chinese state involvement in the reported intelligence affair. Those allegations remain under investigation; they are not established findings of espionage or information compromise.

US export restrictions, Chinese domestic semiconductor development, and Taiwan's industrial position all have continuations that do not depend exclusively on who occupies Nvidia's executive office.

Reinstating Huang would not by itself repeal export restrictions or resolve concerns about improper chip transfers. Removing him would not automatically make those policies wise or their consequences harmless.

The commercial and security questions require evidence and decisions appropriate to their actual functions.

Huang's personal conduct also requires distinctions. Private wrongdoing, workplace conflicts involving relationships with employees, possible security exposure, and the legality of commercial conduct are different matters. The public record has not resolved all of them.

An independent investigation must be able to establish relevant facts and produce consequential findings.

The board must be answerable for its own decisions too.

A governance mechanism that cannot investigate the founder is inadequate. So is one that can remove the founder without understanding what the removal will do to everyone dependent on the company.

Formal independence does not settle either question.

This is where that earlier argument about perfect incentives returns.

Nvidia's internal incentives can receive some information about these relationships. Its customers can complain. Investors can respond. Governments can investigate. Employees can report concerns. Communities can oppose projects.

But the signals arrive through different institutions, at different times, with different powers to change the result.

A company can react intelligently to the information reaching its executives while significant effects remain outside that information.

Nobody owns the entire correction process because nobody possesses the entire field.

## The Path From November 17.

-   **Nvidia should continue under Kress, complete its independent investigations, and carry out a succession.**
-   **Jensen Huang should not return as chief executive.**

That judgment requires more than confidence in Kress or satisfaction with the board's announcement.

The available path begins with the work that is already operating.

Nvidia should maintain lawful customer and supplier commitments, preserve its engineering programs, and continue delivering products whose production and support remain viable. Abruptly abandoning an agreement can damage businesses and people who organized their activities around it.

At the same time, the company should distinguish those commitments from discretionary expansion.

The disclosed neocloud agreements already contain conditions concerning financing, deployment, operational readiness, and acceptance. Nvidia states that some commitments may be reduced or eliminated if the necessary conditions are not met.

That is a real mechanism for changing the path. Kress should use it.

A customer with viable external demand, functioning infrastructure, and adequate financing may deserve continued support. A customer whose purchases rely on further Nvidia assistance without an adequate independent business needs restructuring, different financing, or a carefully managed end to the arrangement.

The distinction cannot be supplied by rhetoric or by a universal prohibition on supplier financing. The contracts, customers, utilization, risks, and obligations have to be examined.

Existing commitments need to be honored or lawfully restructured. The company should not manufacture an unnecessary collapse among customers to make its own balance sheet appear cleaner. But continuing a financing arrangement simply because previous investments make withdrawal uncomfortable can deepen the eventual injury.

The same reasoning applies inside Nvidia.

Kress must have real operating authority. Technical executives need real responsibility for architectures and product decisions. Financial commitments need review capable of changing them. Executive succession must include access to the knowledge and relationships required to carry the company.

The company cannot declare itself independent of Huang while every difficult decision still requires an unofficial conversation with him.

And the board cannot treat the appointment of a successor as completion of that work.

The next month will test whether ongoing operations can continue under the new authority. The next several months will reveal whether major commitments and negotiations can be renewed without Huang. The following product cycle will test whether technical integration survives beyond designs and agreements already substantially prepared under his leadership.

The strongest evidence will come from decisions whose outcome was not fixed before November 11.

The succession should be judged by those continuations.

Some will fail.

A valuable executive may leave. A supplier relationship may weaken. A customer may delay a purchase. A promising startup may lose financing at the wrong moment.

These are possible harms, and actual significant losses should be investigated and addressed.

They do not automatically make reinstatement the preferable course.

The test is whether restoring Huang would preserve essential organized capacity that the available successor arrangement cannot adequately carry, while avoiding greater damage elsewhere.

Today, the public evidence does not establish that necessity.

It establishes a company with formidable existing operating capacity, a highly concentrated founder history, major obligations requiring continuing judgment, and a new leadership arrangement that has not yet been given a fair test.

The defensible move is to make that arrangement capable.

## What We Are Choosing to Lose.

There is a serious case for bringing Huang back.

He may be a better technical strategist than any available successor. He may carry customer confidence that cannot be quickly reproduced. His personal coordination may have helped make extraordinary projects commercially possible. His departure could weaken innovations that matter beyond Nvidia.

Those costs deserve weight.

His reported efforts to tighten financing also complicate any easy story in which the prudent CFO replaces the reckless leather-jacket founder. Huang recognized financial pressure within the system and was already responding to it.

An executive can exercise extraordinary judgment while presiding over a structure that has become too dependent on that judgment.

That is why the decision is difficult.

You could return Huang to the office, recover some of the coordination the company has lost, and attempt to constrain the arrangement around him.

This path still remains conceivable.

But it would require the board to demonstrate that restored executive authority would preserve the relevant capacities without disabling independent correction or recreating excessive dependence.

Huang's experience is evidence of competence. His support among employees, customers, investors, and fellow executives is evidence that his continued participation matters to many of them.

Neither establishes that executive reinstatement is necessary.

The other path accepts an unusually difficult succession and attempts to transfer, distribute, or reconstruct the capacities Huang carried.

That path can be slower. It may be more expensive. It may permanently lose some valuable forms of judgment and coordination.

But it also permits Nvidia to change the relationship between technical contribution, executive authority, financing, and institutional correction.

That possibility has substantial importance in a company whose decisions now affect an enormous region of economic and technological activity.

A founder can be essential to the historical emergence of an organization without remaining its only adequate means of continuation.

The organization's survival should be supported by the people, relationships, knowledge, and institutions it has actually developed.

Where it still depends on the founder, the dependency has to be acknowledged and addressed. Where the founder possesses valuable expertise, the value does not vanish with his title.

Huang may still have meaningful contributions to make here. The form and authority of any future involvement should follow from the investigation and the organization's actual needs.

It should not recreate an executive office whose continued occupancy becomes the condition for every other function to survive.

* * *

## The Ruling.

> **Jensen Huang should not return as Nvidia's CEO.**

Nvidia should preserve the extraordinary productive organization he helped create and make its succession work.

That requires accepting some losses rather than disguising them as temporary inconvenience. The company may sacrifice speed, commercial leverage, valuable relationships, and some measure of strategic coherence while authority is redistributed.

Those costs are real.

They are also part of the price of developing an institution that can continue without requiring one person to remain permanently indispensable.

The financing arrangements deserve the same discipline. Support commitments that carry viable activity. Repair obligations created by past decisions. Restructure commitments that cannot be sustained. Preserve independent sources of capital and customer choice where they can be made effective.

-   Do not treat every existing commercial relationship as something the field must preserve forever simply because Nvidia helped make it possible.
-   Do not treat every financial withdrawal as proof of wisdom because it reduces Nvidia's exposure.

The people and organizations carrying the cost still matter.

A successful transition will leave Nvidia capable of developing new computing systems, supporting its customers, honoring its obligations, and submitting its decisions to correction under leadership that no longer depends upon Huang's personal authority.

It may also leave parts of the wider AI industry more capable of sustaining their own work without continuous support from Nvidia.

Those gains are worth pursuing even if they produce a less spectacular quarter.

The company already possesses much of the capacity needed to begin. What it must now establish is whether that capacity can be coordinated, renewed, and corrected through people other than the founder.

Nadella offered an early piece of evidence. Microsoft was continuing its technical work with Nvidia while respecting the board's authority to decide the succession. His public assessment recognized the significance of the founder and the competence that remained within the company.

Jones made the decision explicit.

The board must now carry it.

Modal Path Ethics has spent considerable time defending exceptional founders against institutions that mistake procedure for competence. That defense still applies to Huang. His achievements are substantial, and the organization would be foolish to pretend otherwise.

But an extraordinary contribution does not confer permanent ownership of the conditions through which everyone else must continue.

This moral framework criticized Huang in September because he treated a powerful system of incentives as though its feedback encompassed the entire field.

His own company now faces a similar temptation.

The founder coordinated an extraordinary institution. The institution became profoundly successful. His continued command consequently appears to some observers as a condition of the institution's survival.

That is a claim about dependency.

It has to be tested.

> **Nvidia should survive Jensen Huang.**

It should do so while preserving the work, carrying the obligations, and repairing whatever the transition damages.

And if a company powerful enough to help reorganize the world's computing infrastructure cannot develop a functioning successor to its own chief executive, the answer is to build that capacity.

We should not need Jensen Huang forever to preserve what Jensen Huang made possible.
