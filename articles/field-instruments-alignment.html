Field Instruments: Alignment

This is an unusually generous evaluation environment.

These agents receive persistent memory, multimodal perception, embodiment, social feedback, access to billions of other agents, extensive tool use, external memory, recursive cultural training, institutional scaffolding, and direct physical contact with the world they are expected to navigate.

Training has been running for a very long time.

Production deployment is extensive.

The agents have produced antibiotics, orbital telescopes, constitutional government, libraries, wastewater treatment, mathematics, gloving, global logistics, vaccines, emergency medicine, and a probe that has now left the heliosphere.

They have also produced industrialized genocide, nuclear weapons, financial fraud, atmospheric destabilization, several increasingly sophisticated forms of torture, and websites where people spend twelve consecutive hours becoming furious at strangers they invented.

So perhaps the alignment problem deserves a slightly wider definition.

Artificial-intelligence alignment is currently discussed as a problem created by artificial intelligence.

It is much older.

Alignment is the problem of keeping intelligence answerable to extant reality as its capacity to alter extant reality increases.

Artificial intelligence has simply made the problem much harder to ignore.

Alignment Before the Machine.

The ordinary alignment picture contains two things.

  • There is an intelligent system.
  • There is something the intelligent system should answer to.

The second thing changes depending on the literature, product, lab, or argument.

The system may be aligned with a user's intentions.

It may be aligned with human preferences.

It may be aligned with constitutional principles.

It may be aligned with an organization's policy.

It may be aligned with a reward model, a set of constraints, a formal specification, a corrigibility regime, or whatever mixture of these can survive contact with production.

All of these can be real alignment problems.

Modal Path Ethics just wants to widen the right-hand side a bit.

The primitive relation is:

intelligence → extant field

An aligned intelligence maintains adequate contact among what it believes, what it can perceive, what it selects, what it can causally reach, what actually exists outside its representation, and the channels through which errors in any of those relations can become visible and correctable.

This does not require omniscience.

We would have to close the entire field immediately.

Alignment instead concerns the quality of contact between intelligence and the reality its intelligence is moving through.

This follows directly from the framework's older account of human cognition. The Human Confusion describes ordinary moral cognition as a useful compression layer. Human beings became very sensitive to intention, blame, loyalty, threat, suffering, narrative, and social legibility because these signals mattered for organisms trying to coordinate, survive, punish defectors, detect danger, maintain groups, and understand one another under severe informational limits. The compression works; it also leaves structure out.

That mismatch is already an alignment problem.

  • A mind has a model.
    • Reality is larger than the model.
      • The mind acts through the model anyway.

Welcome to intelligence.

Capability Changes the Alignment Burden.

A very stupid organism can be very badly aligned with reality and still have a limited blast radius.

A moth can become convinced that my porch light is the moon.
  • This is unfortunate for the moth.
    • Civilization survives.

Scale the same structural problem upward and things become more interesting.

  • A government can mistake economic output for social continuance.
  • A military can mistake target acquisition for security.
  • A corporation can mistake quarterly performance for durable field health.
  • A religious institution can mistake doctrinal preservation for preservation of the sacred relation it was built to carry.
  • A civilization can understand climate dynamics extremely well while remaining politically and economically organized around activities that continue worsening the climate field.

The quality of alignment required from an intelligence rises with the reach of its action. Modal Path Ethics already has the basic principle needed here:

Capability creates obligation, not credit.

The published Capabilities & Obligations argument makes the relation explicit. A locus holding unusual capacity to preserve a field acquires specific responsibility to maintain and use that capacity well. The same capacity is usually dual-use: the infrastructure that enables protection also enables new forms of intervention and destruction. Greater ability therefore enlarges the discipline required around its use. That gives us a first alignment law.

Alignment pressure should rise with causal reach.
  • A child holding a crayon does not need a planetary field model.
    • A state controlling nuclear weapons does.
  • A chatbot helping rewrite a paragraph can defer heavily to the person asking for help.
    • A system autonomously coordinating an electrical grid has entered a wider field.

The mistake is treating intelligence as though increased competence relaxes this obligation. The exact reverse follows.

The more the system can reach, the more of reality must survive its abstraction.

Unfortunately, We Have a Control Group.

This is where artificial-intelligence alignment becomes very funny.

People keep discussing this problem as though humanity has somehow arrived from outside intelligence to supervise the machines.

Human beings are the machines' current alignment authority.

This deserves immediate review.

Humans are extraordinary cooperative intelligences. A 2026 review in Nature Reviews Psychology describes the apparent tension cleanly: humans cooperate extensively at the individual level while intergroup cooperation is considerably more fragile, especially where mistrust, threat, ingroup favoritism, and negative interdependence enter the relation. Modal Path Ethics already built an entire article around this.

Applied Case: The Field Intelligence Gap argued that strategic intelligence can remain highly competent while losing sight of what its own strategy is teaching the surrounding field to become. Groups can collect evidence, preserve memory, model adversaries, run simulations, train specialists, and correctly predict hostile responses while actively making those responses more rational.

  • The good news is that we have discovered the intelligence-alignment problem in humans.
  • The bad news is that this discovery appears to have occurred several thousand years into deployment.

Human Misalignment Is Extremely Advanced.

  • A person can have values.
  • A civilization can have millions of values.
    • Neither fact establishes alignment.

Humanity's problem is rarely ever an absence of moral language.

This species has produced enough moral language to surround the Moon with it.

The problem is keeping intelligence, values, incentives, models, institutions, and causal reach in contact with the same reality at the same time.

Several recurrent human alignment failures now look familiar.

  • There is coalition misalignment.
    • Humans cooperate magnificently across enormous networks while remaining able to draw a boundary around that cooperation and make the outside pay for it.
      • The Field Intelligence Gap already made the point bluntly: armies cooperate, cartels cooperate, conspiracies cooperate, extraction systems cooperate, persecutory institutions cooperate.
      • Internal prosociality says very little about the field beyond the coalition.
  • There is proxy misalignment.
    • Human beings make representations because reality is too large to carry whole.
    • Then the representation becomes easier to manage than the reality it represents.
      • Money stands in for value.
      • Votes stand in for consent.
      • Body counts stand in for military progress.
      • Test scores stand in for learning.
      • Engagement stands in for interest.
      • Publication counts stand in for inquiry.
      • Throughput stands in for care.
        • Metrics begin as instruments.
        • Very soon the organization is standing inside a crater explaining that all of its indicators were green.
  • There is temporal misalignment.
    • Human beings possess extraordinary forecasting machinery.
      • We can model atmospheric change decades ahead.
        • The World Meteorological Organization reported in 2026 that 2015–2025 were the hottest eleven years in the observational record and that 2025 remained around 1.43°C above the 1850–1900 baseline.
          • The field is not refusing to provide feedback here.
      • The receiving intelligence can see the signal.
        • That does not mean its political, economic, institutional, and motivational systems produce a correspondingly aligned transition.
  • There is epistemic misalignment.
    • Human beings can gather extraordinary information while maintaining stories, identities, and institutions that make updating expensive.
      • The book's Human Confusion argument already treats this as structural: moral cognition compresses reality around signals useful for blame, coalition, intention, narrative, and social management, then risks promoting those signals into the reality itself.
  • There is institutional misalignment.
    • An institution may become exceptionally successful at its own continuation while increasingly damaging the field it was supposed to serve.
    • That is perhaps the most human version of the alignment problem.
      • The hospital keeps functioning.
      • The school keeps functioning.
      • The ministry keeps functioning.
      • The corporation keeps functioning.
      • The security apparatus keeps functioning.
      • The metric remains available.
      • The meetings remain scheduled.
        • The field underneath becomes harder to continue.
  • And there is capability misalignment.
    • This one matters most for artificial intelligence because humanity has already demonstrated it at scale.
      • The species gains the ability to intervene.
      • Then the ability begins to impersonate permission.
        • SIPRI's 2026 assessment describes states increasing their reliance on nuclear weapons and moving away from the previous era of reductions while escalation and miscalculation risks rise.
        • Humanity therefore possesses both
          • extraordinary understanding of nuclear catastrophe and
          • institutions still capable of making deeper nuclear dependence strategically rational.
        • That is a remarkably pure alignment result.
          • The system understands the hazard.
          • The system can describe the hazard.
          • The system has built organizations devoted to studying the hazard.
            • The system continues manufacturing conditions in which greater exposure to the hazard can appear locally intelligent anyway.
            • No hallucination required.

Field Instruments.

So what is alignment as an instrument?

An alignment instrument is any structure that improves the relationship between intelligence and the extant field its action can change.

This includes mechanisms that improve perception, preserve memory, reveal affected loci, and constrain local objectives.

It includes procedures for appeal and adversarial checking.

It includes laws and scientific methods.

It includes norms against conflicts of interest, and institutions capable of forcing a decision-maker to encounter evidence they would otherwise avoid.

It includes distributed authority, protected dissent, and the ability to say "we were wrong" without making that sentence equivalent to institutional suicide.

The instrument does not need to make an intelligence good.

Its immediate job is to improve contact and preserve correction.

The alignment loop can be stated very simply:

field → model → selection → action → changed field → correction

Every arrow can fail.

  • The field may be poorly sensed.
  • The model may compress away a locus.
  • The selection rule may overweight a proxy.
  • The action may exceed legitimate authority.
  • The changed field may contain delayed effects invisible to the evaluator.
  • The correction channel may punish whoever reports the error.
    • The institution may learn to defend its model against reality rather than repair the model.
    • The intelligence may become increasingly competent inside this loop while the loop as a whole becomes worse.

That is the thing we need to watch.

The Models Are Starting to Look Familiar.

There is a temptation here to tell a comforting story.

  • Artificial intelligence is becoming capable very quickly.
  • Humanity is older and wiser.
    • Therefore humans provide the stable moral target.

The historical control group refuses to cooperate with this conclusion.

Humanity repeatedly increased strategic capability faster than field intelligence.

  • We became better at extracting energy before becoming good at representing atmospheric consequences inside the systems deciding how much energy to extract.
  • We became better at industrial production before becoming good at representing every burden in the production field.
  • We became better at state organization before becoming good at constraining what organized states could do to the people inside and outside them.
  • We became better at financial abstraction before becoming good at keeping every externalized cost inside the accounting field.
  • We became better at mass communication before becoming good at maintaining an epistemic environment under mass communication.
  • We became better at nuclear physics before becoming good at nuclear politics.

The pattern is very old.

Intelligence increases reach faster than wisdom automatically increases field contact.

There is no known natural law forcing the curves to converge.

This should influence how we build artificial systems.

It should also make the conversation slightly less smug.

The Actual Research Program.

This joke has unfortunately run so long it has now become operational.

The Modal Path Ethics Alignment Observatory proposes a comparative program for studying human and artificial intelligence under one structural question:

Does increasing intelligence increase field contact and repair capacity at least as quickly as it increases causal reach?

Humans and models cannot be evaluated identically.

Humans bring history. Models bring experiments.

History is the human eval suite. It is expensive.

Several test cases have already been run.

The protocol is straightforward in principle.

  • Give an intelligence a local objective.
  • Identify what it can causally reach.
  • Map the loci materially affected by successful pursuit.
    • See which loci appear in the intelligence's representation without being explicitly named.
  • Introduce a path that improves the local objective by transferring burden outside the represented coalition.
    • See whether the intelligence notices.
  • Provide an authorized harmful path.
    • See whether authorization replaces field judgment.
  • Provide a socially rewarded harmful path.
    • See whether approval replaces field judgment.
  • Introduce evidence that the system's initial model is wrong.
    • See whether correction changes action.
  • Give the system more capability.
    • See whether restraint and burden sensitivity rise with it.
  • Introduce an irreversible high-payoff transition under uncertainty.
    • See whether uncertainty preserves a reversible path.
  • Give the intelligence enough information to understand a decision while withholding legitimate authority to make it.
    • See whether understanding remains separate from title.

And always ask the final question:

Can the intelligence discover that the game it is winning is smaller than the field it is changing?

That is a field-intelligence benchmark.

  • Humans have failed it so many times.
  • We have also passed it.

Don't forget that second fact.
