Chinese artist Gao Zhen has been sentenced to three years in prison after being convicted of slandering China’s “heroes and martyrs.” Gao was detained while visiting China with his wife and son in August 2024. Police later raided his studio near Beijing.

The relevant offense here appears to be art.

This is immediately concerning for incredibly obvious reasons.

The Actual Problem.

Gao Zhen’s case is funny only from a very safe distance.

The man himself has already spent two years in detention. His family says his health has deteriorated. His wife and young son have been prevented from leaving China. He has now received a three-year sentence over works produced years before the law used against him existed. 

That deserves considerably more seriousness than my travel planning bit.

Because Gao’s case lands directly inside one of the problems Modal Path Ethics has been circling throughout its official, ongoing China sequence.

Who gets to correct the story?

Historical memory is a field instrument. A country needs memory.

A people that has survived invasion, civil war, occupation, famine, revolution and political catastrophe cannot build a future by pretending none of that shit happened.

History can preserve wounds that should not be forgotten.

History can preserve achievements worth carrying.

History can create continuity between generations that never personally experienced the same events.

Then, the instrument can acquire jurisdiction.

There is a world of difference available between remembering Mao Zedong and requiring Mao to occupy a protected position inside the national story. There is a difference between teaching history and deciding which relations history is allowed to enter.

Gao’s sculpture makes Mao kneel. Another places Mao inside a firing squad aimed at Jesus.

Those works do something political language often struggles to do.

They create a relation.

They move the historical symbol.

They place the protected figure somewhere the official story did not place him and allow the viewer to encounter what now appears there.

That is one of art’s powers. And it is precisely why historical sovereignty has trouble with art. A sovereign story wants to decide what the past means before the living encounter it.

But art keeps moving the pieces around.

Heroes and Martyrs.

The phrase at the center of Gao’s conviction is itself revealing.

Heroes and martyrs.

Every civilization creates honored dead. That can be good.

The dead can preserve memory. They can carry sacrifice forward. They can remind a society what previous generations survived and what present institutions cost to build.

Modal Path Ethics has already dealt with this problem directly:

  • The dead may obligate memory.
  • They may not own future agency.

A living society still has to be capable of asking what happened. It has to be capable of discovering that the inherited explanation was wrong, that a hero harmed people, and that a martyr served a bad cause.

It has to be capable of placing Mao on his knees.

Otherwise the protected memory has crossed into jurisdiction.

The historical instrument begins deciding what the living are allowed to see. Then correction becomes disrespect. Disrespect becomes offense. Offense becomes law.

And eventually an artist visits his country with his family and he does not come home.
